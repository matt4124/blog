#### Overview

As electric vehicle (EV) adoption and rooftop solar generation increase, electricity distribution networks are expected to experience significant changes in both demand and power flow. While EVs can introduce additional demand through charging, their batteries can also provide a valuable source of distributed energy storage.

This project investigated the potential for Vehicle-to-Grid (V2G) technology to support Australian residential electricity networks by allowing EVs to both consume electricity from and supply electricity back to the grid.

I developed a large-scale simulation of an Australian residential distribution network, incorporating household electricity demand, rooftop photovoltaic (PV) generation, EV charging and discharging, and network power flows. The simulation was used to investigate how coordinated V2G operation could influence network performance under different operating scenarios.

#### The Problem

The increasing adoption of renewable energy presents both opportunities and challenges for electricity networks.

Rooftop solar generation can significantly reduce household demand from the grid during periods of high solar output. However, when solar generation exceeds local consumption, excess power can flow back into the distribution network, potentially affecting network voltage and power flows.

At the same time, the increasing adoption of EVs introduces new electrical loads as vehicles are charged from the grid.

V2G technology provides a potential way to coordinate these two trends. Rather than treating EVs solely as additional electricity consumers, their batteries can be used as distributed energy storage systems. EVs could potentially charge when excess renewable generation is available and discharge electricity back to the network when additional support is required.

The objective of this project was to investigate how coordinated EV charging and discharging could affect the operation of a residential distribution network with significant rooftop PV generation.

#### Building the Distribution Network Model

The simulated network was designed to represent an Australian residential distribution system containing both medium-voltage and low-voltage infrastructure.

The model incorporated:

Medium-voltage feeders
Low-voltage distribution feeders
Residential household loads
Rooftop PV systems
EV charging loads
EV battery discharging through V2G

OpenDSS was used to perform the electrical power system simulations, while Python was used to automate simulation workflows, process input data, and analyse the resulting network behaviour.

The overall simulation workflow allowed different combinations of household demand, renewable generation, EV charging, and V2G operation to be investigated.

#### Modelling Residential Demand

Real-world household electricity demand data was incorporated into the simulation to provide more realistic network loading conditions.

The demand profiles were based on five-minute-resolution household electricity data from the NSW South Coast. Using high-resolution demand data allowed the simulation to capture changes in electricity consumption throughout the day rather than relying on simplified constant or average loads.

This was particularly important for investigating V2G operation, as the potential value of EV charging and discharging depends heavily on the timing of household demand and renewable generation.

#### Integrating Rooftop Solar

Rooftop photovoltaic (PV) generation was incorporated into the residential network model to investigate the interaction between distributed renewable generation and EV charging.

The simulation could therefore represent periods where:

Household demand exceeded local PV generation
PV generation supplied a significant proportion of household demand
PV generation exceeded local demand
Excess generation flowed back into the distribution network

These scenarios provided a basis for investigating whether EVs could be coordinated to absorb excess renewable generation or provide additional power to the network when required.


#### Vehicle-to-Grid Operation

EVs were modelled as flexible electrical loads that could either consume power through conventional charging or supply power back to the network through V2G operation.

The simulation workflow allowed different charging and discharging strategies to be investigated.

Conceptually, the EVs could operate in three different ways:

Uncontrolled charging

EVs charge according to their assumed availability and charging requirements without considering network conditions.

Coordinated charging

EV charging is shifted to more suitable periods based on factors such as network demand and renewable generation.

#### V2G operation

EV batteries can both charge and discharge, allowing them to act as distributed energy storage that can respond to network conditions.

Comparing these scenarios provides a way to investigate the potential impact of EV coordination on distribution network performance.

#### Key Takeaways

This project provided experience in modelling the interaction between renewable generation, electric vehicles, distributed energy storage, and electricity distribution networks.

A key challenge was bringing together multiple time-dependent components into a single simulation framework. Household demand, PV generation, EV availability, and network power flows all vary over time, meaning that the impact of V2G cannot be understood by examining any one component in isolation.

The project demonstrated how power system simulation can be used to investigate emerging technologies such as V2G and assess their potential impact on future electricity networks.
