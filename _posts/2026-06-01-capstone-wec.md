## Overview

Wave energy converters (WECs) are renewable energy systems that convert the mechanical energy contained in ocean waves into electrical power.

This project involved developing a simulation model of a wave energy converter to investigate the conversion of wave-induced motion into electrical energy using a linear generator.

![Wave Energy Converter Demonstration](/blog/images/uni_projects/capstonewec/wec_demonstration.gif)

The simulation model was used to investigate the feasibility and performance of the proposed energy conversion system and to understand how the mechanical motion generated by ocean waves could be converted into useful electrical power.

The simulation also provided a way to evaluate the electrical and magnetic design of the generator before physical implementation.



## The Problem

Ocean waves contain a significant amount of mechanical energy, but converting this energy into reliable electrical power presents a number of engineering challenges.

A wave energy converter must capture the irregular motion of ocean waves and convert it into useful electrical energy. This requires the mechanical motion of the system to be closely matched with the electromagnetic behaviour of the generator.

The performance of a linear generator depends on several interacting parameters, including:

- Wave-induced mechanical motion
- Magnet configuration
- Coil geometry
- Number of turns
- Magnetic flux
- Generator speed
- Electrical loading

Changing one parameter can affect the electrical output and overall performance of the system.

The objective of this project was therefore to develop a simulation model that could investigate these relationships and provide insight into the design of a linear generator for wave energy conversion.

## Modelling the Wave Energy Converter

The simulation represented the conversion of mechanical motion into electrical energy through a linear generator.

The general energy conversion process can be represented as:

Ocean Waves → Mechanical Motion → Linear Generator → Electrical Energy

As the wave-induced mechanical system moves, the relative motion between the permanent magnets and coils causes a change in magnetic flux through the coils.

This changing magnetic flux induces an electromotive force (EMF), producing an electrical output that can be analysed using the simulation.

The model allowed the relationship between the mechanical motion of the system and the resulting electrical output to be investigated.


## Electromagnetic Generator Design

A key component of the project was investigating the electromagnetic behaviour of the linear generator.

The generator consisted of a magnet and coil arrangement in which relative motion produced a changing magnetic flux.

The induced voltage was investigated using the relationship between the number of coil turns and the rate of change of magnetic flux:


$$E = -N\frac{d\Phi}{dt}$$


where:

($E$) is the induced electromotive force
($N$) is the number of turns in the coil
($\Phi$) is the magnetic flux

This relationship provided a basis for investigating how changes in the generator design could influence the electrical output.

Parameters such as coil configuration, magnetic field behaviour, and mechanical motion were considered when evaluating the generator design.

## Simulation and Analysis

The simulation was used to investigate how changes in system parameters affected the electrical performance of the generator.

The key outputs investigated included:

**Induced Voltage**

The induced voltage generated by the relative motion of the magnets and coils was analysed to determine how effectively the system converted mechanical motion into electrical energy.

**Electrical Current**

The generated current was investigated under different operating conditions to understand the electrical behaviour of the generator when connected to a load.

**Electrical Power**

The relationship between generated voltage and current was used to evaluate the electrical power output of the system.

The simulation allowed different parameters to be varied and compared, providing a way to investigate design trade-offs before constructing a physical prototype.

## Design Optimisation

One of the main advantages of developing the simulation model was the ability to explore the generator design before physical implementation.

The model could be used to investigate how changes to parameters such as:

Number of coil turns
Magnet configuration
Coil geometry
Generator motion
Wave characteristics
Electrical loading

could influence the resulting electrical output.

This provided a framework for evaluating potential generator configurations and identifying design choices that could improve the performance of the wave energy conversion system.

Simulation-based investigation also reduced the need to physically construct and test every possible design configuration, allowing promising designs to be identified before prototyping.

## Key Takeaways

This project provided experience in modelling the conversion of renewable mechanical energy into electrical energy and investigating the behaviour of a linear electrical machine.

A key challenge was understanding the relationship between the mechanical motion produced by ocean waves and the electromagnetic behaviour of the generator.

The project demonstrated how simulation can be used to investigate complex interactions between mechanical motion, magnetic fields, electrical generation, and system loading.

The simulation provided a foundation for evaluating the feasibility of a linear generator-based wave energy converter and for guiding the electrical and magnetic design prior to physical implementation.
